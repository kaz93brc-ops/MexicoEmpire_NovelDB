## Summary

p.72 begins by completing the p.71 sentence: opposition to centralizing rule and effective central government is framed not only as a defense of local or state autonomy, but also as defense of control over revenues. Hamnett then reads the 1857 Constitution through the previous 75 years of Mexican political experience, especially suspicion of centralism, executive supremacy, and fiscal control.

The constitutional design is summarized as a weak national executive, strong legislature, and federal structure with state governments and state constitutions. Congress was unicameral, departing from the bicameral structures of the 1824 Mexican Constitution and the 1787 U.S. Constitution. Hamnett presents radical Liberals as regarding a senate as an obstacle to social reform, and notes the absence of a presidential veto. He adds that these issues resurfaced after 1867 when Juárez sought to restrain legislative power.

Hamnett states that after Santa Anna abolished federal representative institutions on 1853-12-16, Mexico had no senate until President Sebastián Lerdo re-established it in 1874. He also explains that the 1857 Constitution incorporated the Juárez Law and Lerdo Law and excluded clergy from serving as deputies. The opening articles are summarized as establishing rights and individual guarantees, popular sovereignty, representative democratic federal republicanism, state autonomy in internal government, federal constitutional supremacy, and separation of powers against executive absolutism.

p.73 stresses that federalism could arise from the provinces while state-level politics remained highly centralized. State governors held strong positions and appointed jefes políticos, who functioned as local administrative bosses involved in municipal control, disputed elections, army recruitment, and management of tierras baldías. Hamnett connects their later notoriety especially to the Díaz period.

The page then introduces Emilio Rabasa's later criticism of the 1857 Constitution. Hamnett presents Rabasa as seeing the core defect in imbalance between executive and legislative power, and as criticizing the creation of universal suffrage in a society where, in Rabasa's view, a politically meaningful people or nation did not yet exist. p.73 ends mid-sentence, so the conclusion of this Rabasa criticism is held for the next page.

## Shawcross Cross-Check

- Maximilian期の共和派抵抗は、帝政側から見た単なる反乱ではなく、Juárez側からは1857年憲法と国家合法性の防衛として整理できる。
- 1857年憲法はJuárez側の正統性の根拠である一方、弱い執行権、強い立法権、一院制、拒否権不在という制度的緊張も抱えていた。
- Juárezは憲法擁護者でありながら、1867年以後には立法府を抑えようとする側面が示されるため、単純な憲法主義者像に固定しない。
- 自由主義内部にも、急進改革、一院制支持、議会優位、執行権強化志向という緊張がある。
- 連邦制を掲げながら、州内部では州知事と jefes políticos による中央集権的支配が存在した点は、地方自治・地域権力・国家形成の補正材料になる。
- 聖職者排除、Juárez Law、Lerdo Lawの取り込みはCatholic Churchとの制度的対立に直結するが、近代化対迷信の単純図式にはしない。
- Rabasaの批判は、自由主義憲法の理想と貧困・非識字・先住民社会を含む現実政治とのズレを描く材料になるが、Rabasaの政治的評価を史実認定として扱わない。
