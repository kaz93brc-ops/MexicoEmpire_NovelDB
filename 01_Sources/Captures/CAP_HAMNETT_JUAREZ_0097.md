## Summary

p.188 presents the San Jacinto executions as a shock to diplomatic circles and as a trigger for concern over Maximilian's life from April 1867. Romero reported Seward's fear of massacre at Querétaro while noting the asymmetry that no such solicitude had been shown for Juárez if captured by French forces. Francis Joseph's request moved through the Austrian minister in Washington, the United States government, Seward, and Lewis D. Campbell; Campbell warned Lerdo that repeated executions would damage the Mexican Republic's reputation and U.S. sympathy.

The same page shows Juárez in frequent contact with Escobedo during the siege. His 1867-04-23 instruction separated ordinary soldiers, who should be treated as prisoners, from leaders and commanders whose responsibility would be judged under stricter legal categories; a similar instruction also went to Díaz. Lerdo's reply to Campbell framed Maximilian as a ruler who could have left with the French army but chose continued bloodshed, and the government refused to treat such responsibility as ordinary prisoner-of-war status.

p.189 opens `THE EXECUTION OF MAXIMILIAN`. By mid-May, imperial forces at Querétaro were reduced to about 5,000 while the besiegers had grown to about 30,000. Miramón and Mejía, angry at Márquez's failed relief, advised Maximilian on 1867-05-14 to attempt a breakout. The 1867-05-15 capture itself is already carded in [[FACT_HAMNETT_JUAREZ_2253|FACT_HAMNETT_JUAREZ_2253]] and [[TIME_HAMNETT_JUAREZ_0779|TIME_HAMNETT_JUAREZ_0779]].

After receiving the surrender report, Juárez instructed Escobedo to try Maximilian, Miramón, and Mejía under the law of 25 June 1862. Hamnett emphasizes that a summary execution might have been legally available under the government's wartime logic, but the government chose a court-martial process with public prosecution and defence preparation to avoid the charge of vengeance or haste. On 1867-05-21 the Minister of War conveyed the court-martial policy, a procedure Hamnett treats as virtually guaranteeing death sentences. Escobedo appointed a legally qualified aide as prosecutor, and Maximilian chose Mariano Riva Palacio and Rafael Martínez de la Torre as counsel.
