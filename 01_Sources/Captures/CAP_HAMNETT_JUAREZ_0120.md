## Summary

p.234 completes the unfinished p.233 sentence by saying that if Juarez had lived to the 1875 end of the term begun in 1871, he would have been 69, a plausible age for retirement, and would have had time to pursue constitutional reform and institutional consolidation. Hamnett frames this as counterfactual interpretation, not as an event.

The page then shifts to Juarez's family losses and declining health. Margarita had been ill after her return from exile, Juarez had lost two young sons in New York, Maria Josefa had been seriously ill in October 1870 and recovered, and Margarita died on 2 January 1871 at age 44. Guelatao residents and Bishop Jose Fuentes of Durango wrote to Juarez after Margarita's death.

Hamnett interprets fifteen years of political conflict, the 1871 re-election campaign, and the suppression of La Noria as damaging Juarez's health. Juarez suffered heart attacks on 20 March 1872, 8 July, and the night of 17 July, then died of angina pectoris at 11:30 p.m. on 18 July 1872. The news was conveyed to constitutional successor Sebastian Lerdo de Tejada, and Lafragua was involved in the formal notice of Lerdo's accession.

Hamnett concludes that Lerdo inherited Juarez's unresolved late-presidency problems and that Juarez's death removed the pretext for Diaz's rebellion. Porfirio Diaz arrived in Mexico City on 17 November 1872 and received amnesty from President Lerdo. The chapter text closes with a brief family afterword: Juarez's children before and during the Margarita marriage, Manuela's marriage to Pedro Santacilia, and Benito Juarez Maza's 1911-1912 governorship of Oaxaca.

p.234 lower section and p.235 move into "NOTES AND REFERENCES." Notes 1-20 are visible. Notes 17-20 map to constitutional reform, Tehuantepec route, extraordinary powers, Juarez's death, succession, Diaz amnesty, and family details. Note 15 adds that Governor Castro appointed Benigno Cartas as jefe politico of Tehuantepec. BJDOCS is used multiple times as a source trail; it refers to [[SRC_REF_BJDOCS_TAMAYO|Benito Juarez. Documentos, discursos y correspondencia]], ed. Jorge L. Tamayo, 15 vols, Mexico, 1964-71. Notes were summarized only.
