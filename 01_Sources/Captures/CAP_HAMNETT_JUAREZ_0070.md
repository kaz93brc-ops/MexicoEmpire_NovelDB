## Summary

pp.134-135 show Juárez forced to Saltillo by the French advance while remaining inside Vidaurri's political sphere. Congress had granted Juárez extraordinary powers on 31 May 1863, and Hamnett treats those powers as a tool for strengthening Juárez's authority during occupation.

The same pages show Liberal division: Doblado, González Ortega, and an unnamed Aguascalientes governor viewed Juárez as the obstacle to peace and explored negotiation, while Juárez rejected removal as a path toward European tutelage. Their representatives delivered a letter to Juárez on 9 January 1864 and also approached Miramón, who preferred to await Maximilian.

Patoni and Terrazas refused the manoeuvre against Juárez and supported his stance against Vidaurri and imperial compromise. Terrazas framed the anti-Juárez plan as anti-national, while Vidaurri called for Juárez's removal.

Hamnett then ties Vidaurri's position to 1862-64 border commerce, especially Monterrey and Matamoros interests and the cotton route created by the Union blockade of Texas ports. Juárez's failed personal negotiation with Vidaurri in Monterrey led to the 26 February 1864 separation of Coahuila and Nuevo León, rival-officer pressure against Vidaurri, and Monterrey's brief role as republican capital from 3 August 1864.

After the Juárez government fled on 7 September 1864, Vidaurri recovered Nuevo León and moved toward the Empire through Bazaine's overtures. Maximilian's appointment of Vidaurri to the Imperial Council and temporary finance role is retained as an example of imperial co-optation of regional power.

The page also records Juárez's belief that Vidaurri, Confederates, and Napoleon III's northern designs were linked, and Juárez's hope that the Lincoln administration would use the Monroe Doctrine against France. The new `JUÁREZ IN CHIHUAHUA` section presents the Liberal position as desperate from October 1864, confines the Juárez government to Chihuahua from 12 October 1864 to 10 December 1866, and dates two Paso del Norte residence periods.
