## Summary

pp.216-217 complete the p.215 Zacatecas sentence and move from García de la Cadena, Negrete, and early labor organization into the Nayarit/Lozada agrarian section. Zacatecas is framed as a radical and federalist region hostile to Mexico City politicians, with González Ortega support reinforcing regional distrust.

Hamnett connects Cadena and Negrete from the mid-1860s to the 1886 Díaz defeat and Cadena execution. Their support for urban and peasant movements is kept distinct from their González Ortega ties and Zacatecas regionalism. Hamnett treats the 1868 defeat of Cadena's rebellion as postponing rather than resolving conflict.

The labor discussion links Zacatecas to protection of early factory worker organization after the July 1868 Mexico City textile strike. Mechanization from the 1840s, the Restored Republic's relative openness, the September 1870 Gran Círculo formation, 1871 branches, 1872 pro-administration Liberal infiltration, and later Díaz hostility are summarized without turning the passage into a full transcript.

The new Lozada section uses Nayarit/Tepic as a regional case where community-rights defense, Guadalajara commercial pressure, Liberal privatization, contraband networks, Conservative and Imperial alignments, and Juárez's 1867 compromise converge. Lozada is not treated as simply Conservative, Imperial, Liberal, criminal, or peasant hero; the carding separates those elements.

## Shawcross Cross-Check

### Maximilian Centered Narrative Correction

- Lozada's 3000 men for the Imperial Army suggests that the Empire depended on regional military powers.
- Lozada's Imperial alignment should be read through community rights, subsidies, regional control, and anti-Jalisco interests, not as simple ideological loyalty.
- Lozada's December 1866 defection shows the fluidity of Imperial support as collapse became visible.

### Juárez / Republican View

- Juárez accepted Lozada's recognition despite Hamnett's note that he disliked what Lozada represented.
- Republican reconstruction included compromise with regional armed power, not only constitutional restoration.
- Juárez had to handle peasant communities, regional military power, Liberal Reform, and public order at the same time.

### Liberal / Conservative Binary

- Lozada opposed Liberal disamortization through community-rights defense and therefore moved toward Conservative and Imperial camps.
- His return to the Juárez side prevents a simple Conservative or Imperial label.
- Ogazón's role as a Liberal governor and Lerdo Law beneficiary keeps Liberal reform tied to regional property conflict.

### Catholic Church / Army / Regional Power / Popular Politics

- Catholic Church: not central on this spread.
- Army: federal troops, Imperial Army, Corona's armed conflict, and regional armed power matter.
- Regional power: Zacatecas, Nayarit, Tepic, Jalisco, Guadalajara, and San Blas structure the page.
- Popular politics: peasant community resistance, labor organization, anarchist influence, and factory worker politicization are central.
