## Summary

Hamnett presents the establishment of the Empire as a mismatch between Mexican Conservative expectations and French imperial objectives. The Conservatives expected reversal of the Reform settlement, while Napoleon III's Secret Instructions protected the Catholic Church without disturbing purchasers of nationalized property.

The spread then frames the intervention as a project for a popular-based liberal reform government aligned with Napoleon III's wider policy: restraining the United States, opening markets, and controlling Atlantic-Pacific connections through Mexico and Central America. Forey used French military force to bring together the Assembly of Notables and obtain a call for Maximilian.

Hamnett contrasts Napoleon III and Maximilian's expectation of later public legitimacy with Juárez's 1861 constitutional mandate. The page then moves through Maximilian's acceptance, Church expectations, Napoleon III's advice, the Treaty of Miramar, the military and financial burden of the Empire, Bazaine's dissatisfaction, the Council of State, and Hamnett's periodization of June 1864 to December 1866 as the high point of French success and low point of the juarista cause.
