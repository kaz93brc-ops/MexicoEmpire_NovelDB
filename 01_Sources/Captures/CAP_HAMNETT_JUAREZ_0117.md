## Summary

p.228 resolves the prior unfinished phrase by making the deputies' decision the congressional majority decision that supported Juárez's re-election. Hamnett then argues that La Noria turned against both Juárez and the congressional majority, which gave Juárez a political opening to portray Porfirio Díaz as a military adventurer rather than a constitutional restorer.

The spread places the Díaz brothers in an unfavorable Oaxaca setting. Ixtlán and the surrounding sierra remained loyal to Castro, Juchitán declared for Juárez while waiting for a chance against El Chato, Romero was positioned as the Díaz-side Oaxaca successor, and Alatorre stood with federal troops near the Puebla border awaiting Juárez's order. El Chato feared Ixtlán caciques and sierra militia, while Porfirio tried to keep Fidencio Hernández's militia inactive until the rebellion's outcome was clearer.

After the rebellion began, Romero left Oaxaca for Ixtlán and claimed the acting governorship under Castro and Hernández's protection on 9 November 1871. Juárez had already ordered Alatorre to advance before the Plan's Oaxaca publication. Porfirio Díaz wrongly expected federal troops to defect; when they did not, he left Oaxaca to seek support in central Mexico and reached the Puebla sierra base associated with Méndez and Bonilla. Lucas declared support at Xochiapulco on 26 November.

p.229 covers Juárez's 1 December 1871 speech to Congress, his request for extraordinary powers, the split between lerdistas and porfiristas after Díaz chose armed rebellion, El Siglo XIX's criticism of the Plan, Congress's 80-45 approval of suspension of constitutional guarantees, and Zamacona's refusal to back either Juárez's exceptional powers or Díaz's dictatorship.

The final part turns military. Alatorre's rapid advance defeated Mier y Terán at San Mateo Xindihui on 22 December 1871, opening the way to Oaxaca City where about 2,000 sierra militia under Hernández and Meijueiro waited to join him. Félix Díaz fled south in early January 1872, lost escorts near Ocotlán, missed a ship at Puerto Ángel, hid near Pochutla, and was captured on 22 January by juchitecos led by Albino Jiménez and Benigno Cartas. The page ends mid-sentence and the execution completion is left to the next page.
