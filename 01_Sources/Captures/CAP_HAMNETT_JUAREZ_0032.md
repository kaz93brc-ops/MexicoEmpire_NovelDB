## Summary

p.58-59 continues `THE REVOLUTION OF AYUTLA`. Hamnett presents Ayutla not as a simple Liberal revolution or only a southern revolt, but as a tactical coalition against Santa Anna's personalist government, with popular elements, regional caciques, exile Liberal intellectuals, army defections, and conservative-leaning actors temporarily converging.

p.58 stresses that the movement opened a roughly sixteen-month struggle, included notable popular elements, spread from the south into Oaxaca Mixteca and Michoacán, and connected with Gordiano Guzmán's regional uprising. Hamnett links the popular undercurrent of the Reform era to local grievances over land, water, labor, customary rights, conscription, taxation, Church burdens, religious practice, and autonomy.

p.58 also follows the exile network: Juárez and some Liberal intellectuals were in New Orleans, while Ocampo, Ponciano Arriaga, and José María Mata moved to Brownsville and founded a Revolutionary Junta on 1855-05-22. The Junta contacted Santiago Vidaurri, and the exiles sent Juárez toward Álvarez via Acapulco and Panama, with Santacilia seeing him off on 1855-06-20.

p.59 shows Zuloaga, Haro, Doblado, Degollado, Vidaurri, and the Mexico City garrison as parts of the coalition's success. Haro and Doblado's defense of Church and army protections and support for the 1843 Bases Orgánicas are kept separate from later Liberal reform aims. Hamnett treats Comonfort as a key manager of this heterogeneous coalition and connects its fragility to the 1857 collapse from which Juárez's presidency emerged.

- Vidaurriは、Reform運動の形成者でありながら後に帝政支持へ回る人物として、政治的変転を体現させられる。
- Gordiano Guzmánは、独立戦争世代の地方有力者とReform期の地方反乱をつなぐ人物として使える。
- Ayutla coalitionは、理念で結ばれた革命集団ではなく、Santa Anna打倒という一点で結ばれた不安定な共同戦線として描く。
- HaroやDobladoのような教会・軍保護派がAyutla側に含まれることで、反Santa Anna勢力の複雑さを示せる。
- ZuloagaやHaroのような後の保守派接続人物がAyutla成功に関わることで、政治的陣営が後から再編される感じを描ける。
- JuárezとOcampoの正規軍廃止論は、軍を国家統合の装置と見るか、クーデターの温床と見るかの対立として使える。
- 土地・水・労働・慣習権・徴兵・課税・教会負担・宗教慣習・自治をめぐる地方争点は、改革政治の背後にある生活世界の緊張として描ける。
