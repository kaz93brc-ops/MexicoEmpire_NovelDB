## Summary

Hamnett presents Bazaine as Juárez's most substantial French-side opponent and as a central figure from his July 1863 supreme command through the February 1867 French evacuation. The spread separates Bazaine's later damaged reputation from his importance inside the Mexican intervention.

Hamnett connects Bazaine's Algeria, Spain, Foreign Legion, Bureau Arabe, and information-handling experience to his Mexican anti-guerrilla policy. Bazaine's approach to Mexico is framed as colonial counter-insurgency rather than a purely European conventional campaign.

The page then distinguishes three coercive instruments: Bazaine's 10 April 1864 anti-guerrilla instruction, the imperial decree of 3 October 1865, and Bazaine's 11 October 1865 confidential circular. The decree's responsibility is kept layered: Maximilian blamed Bazaine, Hamnett notes possible Conservative pressure tied to Juárez's 1862 decree, and Juárez treated Maximilian as personally responsible.

p.183 emphasizes the legal-political fight over status. French command treated republicans as bandits, while Juárez insisted that prisoner exchange required recognition as belligerents. The Viesca explanation continues onto the next page.
