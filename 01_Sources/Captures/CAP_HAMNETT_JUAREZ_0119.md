## Summary

p.232 begins the new section "CONSTITUTIONAL REFORM REVIVED." After the collapse of La Noria and Díaz's failure, Juárez returned on 1 April 1872 to the project of reforming the 1857 Constitution. Hamnett treats the revived Senate proposal as evidence that constitutional reform was a central motive behind Juárez's determination to secure re-election in 1871.

Juárez presented Senate creation as a way to make constitutional government workable: it would mediate federal-state conflict, moderate legislative action, and judge offences by state officials. Congress showed little willingness to cooperate. The reply by the President of Congress avoided the reform question and emphasized administrative achievements such as communications, railways, balanced finance, bandit suppression, and European immigration.

At the close of the session on 31 May 1872, Juárez regretted that Congress had not discussed constitutional reform and hoped the next session would take it up. He also urged continued attention to the inter-oceanic route across the Isthmus of Tehuantepec, connecting this spread to the previous Isthmus and Oaxaca material.

p.233 then turns from reform to public security. Díaz's defeat had not restored peace: disorder or rebel groups remained in Sinaloa, the northern frontier, the Puebla sierra, and Yucatán. On 1 April 1872 Juárez asked Congress to extend extraordinary faculties granted during La Noria, including suspension of constitutional guarantees and power to declare a state of siege where required. He argued that the powers were for restoring order rather than raising extraordinary taxes or forced loans, and pointed to rapid lifting of emergency measures in Aguascalientes as evidence of restraint.

Despite opposition from Lafragua and others, Congress approved the extension on 17 May 1872 by 95 votes to 37. p.233 then begins "THE DEATH OF JUÁREZ - JULY 1872," framing the 1871 term as scheduled to end on 30 November 1875 and introducing a counterfactual question about how much time Juárez might have had for constitutional reform had he completed the term. The sentence continues onto the next page.
