## Summary

p.80 completes the previous page's Miguel Lerdo sentence: Lerdo's January 1857 resignation is linked to his serious consideration of challenging Comonfort in the presidential election planned for June. The page then shifts to the instability of the 1857 constitutional experiment: promulgation of the Constitution brought armed clashes in strongly Catholic regions, federal policy depended on state militias, and the regime's survival ultimately depended on the regular army and Félix Zuloaga's Mexico City command.

Hamnett presents the Comonfort election and the 17 September cabinet resignation as signs that electoral victory did not resolve Liberal division. Silíceo's account depicts outgoing ministers as skeptical of constitutional government and inclined toward reform by presidential decree, while Knapp is introduced for Sebastián Lerdo's alleged skepticism about the Constitution's workability.

p.81 develops Hamnett's interpretation of the 1857 Constitution's central problem: weak executive power, strong legislature, strong state governments, and governor-shaped elections. Hamnett separates his view from Bulnes's later reading, treating the autumn 1857 elections as a defeat for national Liberal leadership rather than for the Constitution or reform itself. The page ends with state radical Liberals using central weakness to pursue Church property and federal revenues.
