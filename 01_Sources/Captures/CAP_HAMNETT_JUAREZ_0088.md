## Summary

Hamnett frames Napoleon III's Mexican policy as part of French global and imperial strategy in the 1850s and early 1860s. Mexico is compared with Algeria and Indo-China as a target of French imperial policy, while Tehuantepec is treated as a strategic transoceanic project linked to Suez and Atlantic-Pacific planning.

The spread then connects Tehuantepec to Oaxaca Liberal press criticism, Maximilian's 1866 preference for a French or European-led project, older French interests in Spanish America, and the opportunity created by the U.S. Civil War. Hamnett stresses that Napoleon III seems not to have studied Juárez's career or political importance, while Juárez understood both French policy shifts and the volatility of Mexican politics.

On p.171, Hamnett links the Allied split at Veracruz, Puebla's 5 May 1862 effect, Forey's 10 June 1863 entry into Mexico City, and the July 1863 Union victories to the viability problem of the Mexican Empire. The new section begins with Forey transferring sovereignty to the Regency Council, naming Salas, Almonte, and Labastida; the sentence continues on the next page.
