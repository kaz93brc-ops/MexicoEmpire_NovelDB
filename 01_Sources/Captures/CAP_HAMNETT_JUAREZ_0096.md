## Summary

Miramón returns from European exile to command imperial resistance, while Hamnett presents Maximilian as still imagining liberal institutional solutions but increasingly constrained by Mexican Conservatives.

The spread follows Bazaine's withdrawal priority, the 14 January 1867 junta decision, the 5 February evacuation order, Miramón's Zacatecas threat, Juárez's escape, and the reported discovery of Maximilian's execution order.

San Jacinto shifts the military balance: Escobedo defeats Miramón, captured foreign volunteers are executed, and Joaquín Miramón is later executed. Republican advances then converge through Guadalajara, San Luis Potosí, Guanajuato, Morelia, Cuernavaca, Querétaro, and Puebla.

Maximilian leaves Mexico City for Querétaro, entrusts abdication handling to Lacunza, appoints a Regency Council, and becomes trapped with the Imperial Army until the 15 May 1867 capture and surrender at Cerro de las Campanas.

- Bazaine prioritizing evacuation and direct contacts over imperial responsibility.

- Maximilian's Regency Council can be written as idealism, denial, or institutional habit, but Hamnett's evaluation is negative.
