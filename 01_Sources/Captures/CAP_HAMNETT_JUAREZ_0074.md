## Summary

Hamnett closes Chapter 6 by presenting Juárez's severity as double-sided: the harshness he showed opponents is paired with harshness toward himself. The point should be treated as Hamnett's interpretation, not as a direct window into Juárez's inner speech.

Hamnett then summarizes Juárez's wartime aim as defeating Maximilian's Empire, making the French Intervention fail irretrievably, humiliating European imperial ambitions, vindicating the Republic, and forcing Mexicans themselves to defend independence. He adds that the symbolic weight was larger because this occurred in an era marked by European racial hierarchy and because Mexico was a non-European country with a mestizo-Indian majority.

For Hamnett, the 1863-67 issue was whether Mexicans could decide Mexico's future without European imperial political tutelage. The conclusion keeps Juárez's legal-republican language beside a very hard political will; the short phrase `politician made of pure steel` is retained only as a brief original-language marker.
