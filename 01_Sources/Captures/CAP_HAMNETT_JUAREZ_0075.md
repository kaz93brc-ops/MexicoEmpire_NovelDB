## Summary

p.144 is source-trail material. Notes 31-36 point to Cadenhead, Archivo Juárez MSJ, BJDOCS, Almada, Dabbs, and APBJPS as bases for the closing Chapter 6 issues around González Ortega, Juárez government documentation, Terrazas and Chihuahua, French military occupation, and Juárez family/Santacilia correspondence. APBJPS is expanded here as Archivo Privado de D. Benito Juárez y D. Pedro Santacilia, and BJDOCS as Benito Juárez. Documentos, discursos y correspondencia; Archivo Juárez MSJ and APBJPSI remain verification items.

p.145 starts Chapter 7 by placing Juárez's career in North American as well as Latin American history. Hamnett argues that the 1857-67 Mexican struggles affected the balance of power in North America and European powers' relation to the American continent, and that the United States Civil War strengthened the significance of Mexican events.

The opening of `TERRITORIAL CESSION AND TRANSIT RIGHTS` presents postwar Mexico-U.S. relations as uneasy. Hamnett attributes that unease to Mexican suspicion of further United States territorial and transit-right designs, continued Democratic Party pressure with southern connections, and Mexican government perceptions that southern expansionists stood behind the War of 1846 and later pressures.
