## Summary

Hamnett treats the 14 August 1867 Convocatoria as a turning point in which Juárez tried to move constitutional reform beyond party and intellectual circles to the electorate. The proposed route provoked charges of betrayal, exposed existing Liberal divisions, drew on the still-held 1863 extraordinary powers, and produced state-level conflict when Puebla and Guanajuato governors refused publication. The new section then reframes opposition after 1867 as a long-standing Liberal and constitutional conflict rather than a simple Conservative reaction.
