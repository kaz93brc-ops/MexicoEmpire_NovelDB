## Summary

Hamnett resolves the previous page's unfinished thought by identifying the 17 July 1861 two-year debt-payment suspension as the decision that directly triggered European Intervention, while retaining the postwar reconstruction and fiscal survival context of the Juárez government.

The spread then separates British, Spanish, and French motives. Britain is presented mainly through finance and debt enforcement; Spain through Bourbon prestige, Cuba, U.S. influence, and possible dynastic calculations; France through Jecker claims, Morny, Saligny, exile-monarchist pressure, Eugénie's circle, and Napoleon III's emerging imperial design.

The page also complicates Conservative and monarchist assumptions: Miramón, Prim, and later Bazaine warned that Mexico lacked a real monarchist party, while Montluc in Paris warned that a French campaign would require large forces and heavy expense.
