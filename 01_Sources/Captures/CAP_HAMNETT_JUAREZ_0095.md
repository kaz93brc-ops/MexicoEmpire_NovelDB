## Summary

p.184 first completes the Viesca explanation from the previous spread. Juárez's prisoner-exchange position is treated as a law-of-war and sovereignty argument, not a simple humanitarian exchange proposal.

Hamnett then moves from Bazaine's response to the new section "THE COLLAPSE OF THE EMPIRE". He presents French withdrawal after the U.S. Civil War as unavoidable, while avoiding a one-cause explanation based only on a likely U.S.-French war.

The spread follows the withdrawal problem from Napoleon III's timetable, through Bazaine's low confidence in the Empire, republican advances in the north, the August 1866 support-withdrawal message to Maximilian, and the September-December evacuation acceleration.

p.185 shifts the collapse story to Maximilian's decision-making: Bazaine and Maximilian distrusted each other, Carlota's Paris criticism weakened Bazaine, Maximilian relied on court advisers, and Orizaba became the setting for his five-week abdication crisis and eventual decision to remain.
