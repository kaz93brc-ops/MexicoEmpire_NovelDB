## Summary

p.56 first completes the p.55 Oaxaca crisis sentence: Díaz's seizure of Oaxaca civil authority is tied to Juárez's request, so the earlier continuation card is updated rather than duplicated. Hamnett then closes the Liberal constellation section by stressing Juárez's tactical flexibility, his non-subordination to Oaxaca radicals, and his attempt to stand above or carefully move between factions while holding firm to constitutional government, law, and civil power.

p.56 bottom and p.57 open `THE REVOLUTION OF AYUTLA`. Hamnett places Juárez's national emergence after Ayutla and frames the movement as southern, locally rooted, and connected to villages and non-elite social and ethnic groups, not simply as an elite Liberal manifesto.

p.57 explains the Plan of Ayutla as initially born from local cacique hostility to central power. Alvarez and Comonfort shaped a pronouncement at Hacienda La Providencia, Villarreal proclaimed it in the Ometepec district, and only Comonfort's Acapulco revision gave it clearer Liberal coloring. Hamnett contrasts Comonfort's moderate Liberalism with Alvarez's flexible popular alliances, including the elite fear of a caste element that Juárez did not share in the same way.

## Key Points

## Shawcross Cross-Check

- Hamnett p.56 can qualify a Shawcross-style Juárez-as-republican-resistance focus by showing Juárez trying to place national interest above party.
- The post-1867 Liberal Party obstacle theme helps stage republican victory as followed by internal conflict.
- Ayutla's origins were not initially a fully anti-Church Liberal program, correcting a simple modernization narrative.
- Comonfort's moderate Liberalism complicates Conservative or imperial assumptions that Liberalism meant direct anti-Church radicalism.
- Alvarez's popular alliances and elite fear of caste elements show social and ethnic tension inside republican politics.
- Juárez's lack of fear toward the popular element should be handled as political calculation and composure, not hero worship.

## Creative Use

- Juárez can be written as a practical politician moving through factions with constitutional, legal, and civil principles as anchors.
- The Liberal Party as obstacle prevents the restored republic from reading as a simple liberation bloc.
- Ayutla can be staged through southern rebellions, villages, and non-elite mobilization rather than only elite declarations.
- Comonfort can carry moderate reform tension: constitutionalist and reformist, but wary of army dissolution and direct Church attack.
- Alvarez can use popular forces while also containing their autonomy.
- Fear of a caste element can be shared elite anxiety crossing Liberal / Conservative lines.
