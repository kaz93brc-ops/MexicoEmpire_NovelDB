## Summary

pp.218-219 complete the p.217 unfinished military-district phrase by explaining Juárez's placement of Nayarit/Tepic under federal direct jurisdiction. Hamnett treats this as a practical move that denied Pedro Ogazón and Ramón Corona an immediate opportunity to attack Manuel Lozada after the Empire's collapse, leaving Lozada protected by a tacit accommodation with Juárez.

The remaining Nayarit passage follows Lozada's circulars to Tepic villages, especially the 12 April 1869 circular authorizing village direct action in defense of land rights. Hamnett then frames Juárez's lifetime as a period when neither Guadalajara nor Mexico City could easily remove Lozada. After Juárez's death, the Lerdo administration authorized Corona's final campaign, Lozada invaded central Jalisco with a large force, was defeated and handed over to enemies, and was executed on 19 July 1873.

The lower p.218 and p.219 material contains Chapter 9 "NOTES AND REFERENCES" notes 1-23. The notes are treated here as a source map only; note bodies are not transcribed.

## Shawcross Cross-Check

### Maximilian Centered Narrative Correction

- Lozada remained important after the Empire's collapse as a Nayarit/Tepic regional military and agrarian actor.
- Republican authorities did not simply eliminate all former Imperial collaborators; Juárez temporarily accommodated Lozada to stabilize the region.
- Post-Imperial order restoration included bargains between the center and armed regional powers.

### Juárez / Republican View

- Juárez's reconstruction depended on negotiation with hostile or semi-autonomous regional power, not only legal restoration.
- Federal jurisdiction over Nayarit can be read as a pragmatic way to restrain Corona and Ogazón while containing Lozada.
- The move from Juárez's restraint to Lerdo's authorization of Corona marks a political transition after Juárez's death.

### Liberal / Conservative Binary

- Lozada's Imperial and Conservative associations must be read together with community land-rights defense and regional order.
- Lerdo's campaign can be read as Liberal state reassertion over regional armed and village-based power.
- Juárez, Corona, Ogazón, and the Lerdo administration did not share one uniform Liberal response to Lozada.

### Catholic Church / Army / Regional Power / Popular Politics

- Catholic Church is not central on this spread.

- Regional power: Nayarit/Tepic, Jalisco, Guadalajara, and Mexico City define the conflict.
