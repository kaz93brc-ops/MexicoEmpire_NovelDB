## Summary

Hamnett presents Juárez's July 1867 return to Mexico City as the start of a difficult restored-republican settlement, not a simple triumphal close. The spread interprets Juárez's postwar programme as moderate and centralizing, stresses the return of local conflicts after the Intervention, and argues that Liberal Party division and provincial resistance shaped national politics more than defeated Conservatism did.
