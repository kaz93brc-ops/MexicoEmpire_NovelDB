# Mexican Congress

## Historical Role

Hamnett p.53 says the Mexican Congress voted in 1869 to recognize Cuban insurgents as belligerents.

## Creative Use

Use to show Cuban support as institutional politics, not only personal sympathy.

## Open Questions

## Hamnett 1994 p.126-127 Notes

Hamnett pp.126-127 presents Congress as a site of opposition to Juárez after the 1861 election, as the institution Juárez feared might be transformed into a dictatorial Convention, and as a body where radical deputies could support Juárez.

## Hamnett 1994 pp.154-155 Wyke-Zamacona Rejection Notes

Hamnett pp.154-155 records the Mexican Congress unanimously rejecting the Wyke-Zamacona Treaty, a rejection Hamnett presents as politically isolating the Juárez government and ending U.S. loan discussions.

## Hamnett 1994 p.128-129 Notes

Hamnett pp.128-129 places Congress at the center of the 1861 crisis: 51 deputies sought Juárez's resignation, 52 supported him, and Hamnett later argues that Congress interpreted the 1857 Constitution as moving Mexico toward a parliamentary system.

## Hamnett 1994 p.130 Notes

Hamnett p.130 shows Congress reluctantly conceding extraordinary powers on 1861-12-11 and later suspending constitutional guarantees on 1862-05-03 under the pressure of foreign intervention.
