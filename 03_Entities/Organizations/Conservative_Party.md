## Hamnett 1994 p.68 Notes

Hamnett p.68 argues that the social consequences of the Lerdo Law gave local conservatives an opportunity to use popular dissatisfaction with the Liberal regime. Lerdo, from the puro side, thought Comonfort should have destroyed the Conservative Party rather than conciliated opponents.

## Hamnett 1994 p.90 Notes

Hamnett p.90 distinguishes the Conservative Party from the Catholic Church itself, treats it as a secular political grouping associated with Alamán in the late 1840s, and explains its use of religious feeling as political mobilization against Liberal reform.

## Hamnett 1994 p.97 Puebla Rebellion Notes

Hamnett p.97 treats the Puebla rebellion as more than clerical reaction: rebel leaders sought a provisional president above party, Haro searched for an alternative to Comonfort's government, and Conservative soldiers such as Osollo and Miramón entered the crisis.
