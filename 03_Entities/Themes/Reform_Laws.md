# Reform Laws

## Historical Role

Hamnett p.104-105 treats the Veracruz Reform Laws as the legislative package issued from July to December 1859 during the Reform War, after repeated delays, cabinet conflict, and state-level pressure.

Hamnett p.106-107 adds that the 1859-07-12 Veracruz Reform Laws implemented many Manifesto principles through executive action, while their immediate enforcement depended on Liberal territorial control.

Hamnett pp.108-109 further shows the package reaching marriage, Civil Registry, burial, public religious ceremony, official celebrations, clerical public markers, clerical taxation, and religious belief freedom.

Hamnett pp.110-111 adds the episcopal Pastoral Letter response, the end of Comonfort-style Church incorporation, and the link from religious liberty to Protestantism and Liberal national formation.

Hamnett pp.112-113 adds the first national enforcement conditions after Liberal victory, clergy fines, high Church expulsions, Zarco's defense, and the fiscal problem of nationalized Church property.

Hamnett p.115 notes 35-42 add source locators for Veracruz documents, Juárez to Doblado, Protestantism, January 1861 Church measures, Zarco, and Church-property/debt issues.

## Creative Use

Use as a legislative turning point tied to Veracruz politics, Church property, civil authority, wartime finance, and Liberal factional conflict.
