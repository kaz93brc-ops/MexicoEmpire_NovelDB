# Liberal Factionalism

## Historical Role

Hamnett p.54-55 presents Liberalism as internally divided among Oaxaca factions, progressives, moderates, and extremists.

## Creative Use

Use to avoid portraying republicans as a single coherent bloc.

## Open Questions

- Distinguish local Oaxaca faction terms from national progressive and moderate labels.

## Hamnett 1994 p.60-61 Notes

Hamnett p.60-61 traces Liberal factionalism back to the early Álvarez cabinet, where puros around Álvarez and Ocampo clashed with moderates around Comonfort. Hamnett also connects these divisions to the later failure of the Liberal Party as a governing party and to Porfirian authoritarianism after 1884.

## Hamnett 1994 pp.242–243 Conclusion Closing Notes

Hamnett concludes that Conservative defeat and official Liberal ideology did not create coherent one-party government: factionalism, personalism, extra-constitutional action, congressional obstruction, and armed regional competition continued.
