# Presidentialism

## Historical Role

Theme for accusations that Juárez concentrated executive authority against state governors or Congress, especially after the Veracruz regime returned to Mexico City.

## Creative Use

Use to distinguish constitutional legitimacy from executive dominance in scenes where Juárez's opponents criticize his governing style.

## Open Questions

- Confirm who used the charge, in which documents or debates, and whether Hamnett adopts or reports the term.
- Distinguish "presidentialism" from lawful emergency powers and from later Porfirian executive dominance.

## Hamnett 1994 p.126-127 Notes

Hamnett pp.126-127 connects presidentialism criticism to Juárez's election legitimacy, congressional opposition, the Vidaurri warning about a dictatorial Convention, and González Ortega's Supreme Court / War Ministry position.

- Cautions: Do not treat the charge of presidentialism as proof of dictatorship without separating opposition rhetoric, Hamnett interpretation, and verifiable institutional acts.

## Hamnett 1994 p.128-129 Notes

Hamnett pp.128-129 connects presidentialism to the near-even congressional division, state-governor support for Juárez, juarismo, and Congress's interpretation of the 1857 Constitution as limiting the presidency.
