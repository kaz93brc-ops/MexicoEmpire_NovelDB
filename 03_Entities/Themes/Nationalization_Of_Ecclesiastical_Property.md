# Nationalization Of Ecclesiastical Property

## Historical Role

Hamnett p.103 describes Vidaurri in Coahuila as an early legislator and implementer of full nationalization of Church property.

Hamnett p.107 treats the Veracruz nationalization and Church-state separation law of 1859-07-12 as a central Reform Law that went beyond the 1856 Lerdo Law by publicly selling Church property and directing the proceeds to the national treasury.

Hamnett pp.112-113 says the Juárez government had not abandoned using nationalized ecclesiastical properties as collateral for U.S. loan negotiations, while also judging that post-1856 Church property measures failed to produce the expected fiscal results.

Hamnett p.115 note 42 points to BJDOCS volume 4 and Knowlton p.88 as locators for the Church-property, U.S. loan, external debt, and fiscal-limit cluster.

## Creative Use

Use to separate state-level property nationalization from earlier disamortization mechanisms and from Juárez's direct initiative.

## Open Questions

- Confirm law name, date, jurisdiction, and how it differed from disamortization.
- Confirm the official Spanish title and whether Hamnett's phrasing refers to one combined law or two related laws.
- Confirm note 42 for the U.S. loan-collateral idea, external-debt problem, and fiscal-result evaluation.
