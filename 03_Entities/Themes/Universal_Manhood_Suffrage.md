# Universal Manhood Suffrage

## Historical Role

Hamnett p.74 treats universal manhood suffrage as a principle that expanded the practical problem of how Mexican republican elections were run under the 1857 Constitution.

Hamnett p.73 introduces Rabasa's criticism that universal suffrage was placed in a society Rabasa considered politically unprepared; that criticism is recorded as Rabasa's later evaluation, not as DB fact.

## Creative Use

Use as an ideal that characters can invoke while local officials, electoral colleges, and factional pressures shape the actual outcome.

## Hamnett 1994 p.77 Notes

Hamnett p.77 says the February 1857 Constitution established the principle of adult male suffrage, while Oaxaca used indirect elections to work around distance, mistrust, hostility, and limited political knowledge.
