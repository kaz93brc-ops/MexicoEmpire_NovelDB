## Hamnett 1994 p.68 Notes

Hamnett p.68 places Lerdo as a leading puro who wanted reform legislation and disamortization advanced through state power. Lerdo criticized Comonfort's conciliatory line, left the government on 1857-01-03, and for a time considered challenging Comonfort in the planned presidential election under the new Constitution.

## Hamnett 1994 p.103 Notes

Hamnett p.103 says Miguel Lerdo reached Guadalajara at the beginning of December 1858 and advocated dictatorship as a means to achieve Liberal objectives. He pressed Juárez for Church-state separation and confiscation of ecclesiastical property without compensation, and later split with Ocampo over the purpose of disamortization after the Juárez government reached Veracruz.

## Hamnett 1994 p.121 Veracruz Regime Notes

Hamnett p.121 presents Miguel Lerdo as the figure many radicals regarded as a more natural Liberal leader than Juárez, and as finance and development minister from January 1859 to June 1860. Lerdo's rivalry with Ocampo divided the cabinet but, in Hamnett's interpretation, strengthened Juárez's claim to leadership.
