## Hamnett 1994 p.119 Notes

Hamnett p.119 makes Degollado a crucial counterweight to Vidaurri: he was Michoacán governor, accepted supreme command after Parrodi, and is described through Guanajuato origin plus ties to Ocampo and Benito Gómez Farías.

## Hamnett 1994 p.122-123 Notes

Hamnett pp.122-123 places Degollado in three roles: correspondent with Ocampo whose captured letters were published by Conservatives, commander sent by Juárez to reduce Vidaurri to obedience, and later mediation supporter in contact with George Mathews.

## Hamnett 1994 p.124 Notes

Hamnett p.124 makes Degollado's Mathews connection a direct rupture point with Juárez: Degollado authorized publication of the Mathews mediation plan, Juárez protested, and Hamnett interprets Juárez as losing confidence in Degollado.

## Hamnett 1994 p.126 Notes

Hamnett p.126 records Degollado's death in an ambush on 1861-06-15 and treats his removal from politics as part of the leadership vacuum that elevated González Ortega as Juárez's rival.
