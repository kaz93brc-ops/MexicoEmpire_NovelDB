# Ignacio Alatorre

## Historical Role

Hamnett pp.204-205 names Ignacio Alatorre among the army officers to whom Juárez delegated authority against provincial rebellion, and p.204 links him to Juárez's re-establishment of control in Oaxaca.

## Creative Use

Use as a military intermediary between the central government and Oaxaca local power.

## Hamnett 1994 p.209 Puebla Notes

Hamnett p.209 says Juárez appointed Alatorre chief of operations in Puebla rebel districts, characterizes his method as brinkmanship to avoid bloodshed, and links him to Zacapoaxtla reporting and Lucas's non-entry into rebellion.

## Hamnett 1994 pp.210-211 Notes

Hamnett pp.210-211 has Alatorre defeat Negrete at Tepeji, join Cravioto in seeking federal payment for Puebla operations, and later become Military Commander and Governor after the La Noria rebellion collapsed.

## Hamnett 1994 pp.228-229 Oaxaca Advance Notes

Hamnett pp.228-229 places Alatorre near the Puebla-Oaxaca border awaiting Juárez's order, then advancing rapidly enough to defeat Mier y Terán at San Mateo Xindihui on 22 December 1871 and open the route to Oaxaca City.

## Hamnett 1994 pp.230-231 Oaxaca Political Control Notes

Hamnett pp.230-231 has Alatorre remove Romero from the acting governorship on 8 January 1872, take military and political control, nominate Castro as provisional governor, and appear in Hamnett's list of commanders on whom Juárez could rely.
