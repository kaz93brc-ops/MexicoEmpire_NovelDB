# Veracruz Reform Laws 1859-07-12

## Historical Role

Hamnett p.107 treats 1859-07-12 as the first enactment of the Veracruz Reform Laws, by which the Veracruz Liberal government began to implement many principles announced in the 1859-07-07 Manifesto.

Hamnett pp.108-109 extends the package through civil marriage, Civil Registry, burial and funeral jurisdiction, official celebrations, public religious ceremony regulation, clerical taxation, and liberty of religious belief.

Hamnett pp.110-111 records the episcopal Pastoral Letter response and links religious liberty to Protestantism, United States influence, and Liberal national formation.

Hamnett pp.112-113 adds the postwar stage in which Liberal victory and return to Mexico City made national enforcement possible, while clergy fines, high Church expulsions, Zarco's defense, and public-debt pressure shaped the laws' immediate political context.

Hamnett p.115 notes 35-42 add source locators for 1859 Veracruz documents, Juárez to Doblado, Church property, Protestantism, January 1861 Church measures, Zarco, and debt.

## Creative Use

Use as the wartime legislative turn from manifesto language to administrative decree, while keeping fiscal policy, Church-state separation, and coercive wartime governance distinct.
